# PRD 01: Feed the Goose v1

Status: draft, written 2026-11-26 for the v1 build. Owner: Matt.

Feed the Goose is a habit app for iOS. A goose chases you through the day. Each time you log
part of a habit you throw it a crust and gain ground. Finish every habit and the goose is fed.
Let your lead run out and it catches you.

This PRD says what v1 does and how to tell that it works. The
[design doc](../../technical/tdds/01-v1.md) says how it is built, and the
[work breakdown](../../technical/tickets/01-v1.md) splits it into tickets. Terms are defined in
[CONTEXT.md](../../CONTEXT.md).

## Product defaults Matt can veto

Matt has not answered these. The build uses them until he does. The technical defaults are
listed at the top of the design doc.

| Default | Source |
| --- | --- |

| The clips are **Snap** for a gain, **Feast** for a complete day and **Peck** for a caught day. | Decisions doc §G; prototype `05-animations` |

| Undoing a log on a complete day changes the streak, and the public profile, **at once**. | API spec open question 3 |
| On a user's **first day the goose starts walking when the first habit is created**, not at the day's start, so nobody is caught minutes after signing up. | New; the spec did not cover it |

## Who it is for

Matt, on his own iPhone, on day one. Sign-up is limited to an allowlist of emails that starts
with his. Other people are a later decision, and nothing in v1 may make that harder.

## Goals

1. **The chase makes the day legible.** At a glance you know how far ahead you are and when
   the goose would catch you.
2. **Logging takes one tap.** You can log a chunk from the Home Screen without opening the app.
3. **Partial progress counts.** Ten of fifty pushups moves you ahead.
4. **The chase is the same everywhere.** The app, the widgets, Shortcuts, an MCP client, a
   script and the public profile all show one chase, because the server works it out.
5. **You can show someone.** A public profile shows your streak and your chase without your
   habits.

## What tonight's build must show

The build's target is **an end-to-end demo recorded in the iOS Simulator against a backend
running locally**. Deployment is out of scope for now. The demo is one recording, made from
real simulator captures, that shows:

1. dev sign-in and setting up three habits;
2. the Today chase, a log that plays Snap and raises the lead, and an undo;
3. a log from the Feed widget on the Home Screen, with the Chase widget following;
4. a log made through MCP from outside the phone, appearing in the app;
5. finishing every habit, which plays Feast;
6. a second, seeded user being caught, which plays Peck;
7. a log made while the backend is unreachable, queued and then delivered;
8. the public profile page in a browser, with no habit names on it.

Simulator evidence is evidence, not acceptance. Matt's check on a real phone comes with the
first TestFlight build, and it is the only thing that accepts widget buttons, the Lock Screen,
push delivery and offline behavior.

## Scenarios and acceptance criteria

Each scenario is written for the goose. The numbers in the examples come from one day: three
habits, a day from 6:00 am to midnight, and a full starting lead.

### S1. Sign in

- In a debug build, I can sign in with the dev sign-in by entering an email.
- An email that is not on the allowlist is refused with a message that says so.
- I stay signed in across launches. The widgets and Shortcuts actions work without asking me
  to sign in again.
- Signing out removes the session from the phone and revokes it on the server.
- *Release, not in the demo:* I sign in with Auth0 in an in-app browser sheet.

### S2. Set up habits

- After my first sign-in the app asks for my habits before it shows a chase.
- A habit has a name, a unit, a target and a chunk, such as Pushups, reps, 50 and 10.
- I can add up to 8 habits, reorder them, rename them, change a target and archive one.
- A habit I add before my first log of the day counts today. Otherwise it starts tomorrow, and
  the app tells me which.
- A new target and an archived habit take effect tomorrow, and the app tells me so.
- On my first day the goose starts walking when I create my first habit.

### S3. See the chase

- Today shows the scene: the goose behind me, the sky for the time of day, and a headline such
  as "80 steps behind you".
- Below it, each habit shows its progress ("20 of 50 reps"), a chunk button ("+10") and a
  finish button ("Finish 30").
- The lead falls as time passes without my doing anything.
- When the lead is low, the headline changes color to warn me.
- If the app could not refresh, it shows that the chase is out of date, not a wrong lead.

### S4. Log, and undo

- Tapping "+10" on Pushups plays Snap, raises the lead, and writes a line such as "10 reps
  logged. The goose stops for the crust and you gain 7 steps."
- Tapping "Finish 30" logs what is left of the habit and marks it done.
- I can log any other amount for a habit.
- Logging more than is left of a habit earns only what was left.
- "Undo" takes back my last log and the lead falls back.
- With Reduce Motion on, the scene shows the clip's last frame instead of playing it.

### S5. Feed the goose

- When my last habit reaches its target, Feast plays, the headline says the goose is fed, and
  the log buttons are gone.
- The streak goes up by one.
- Tomorrow the goose starts 100 steps back.
- If I undo the log that finished the day, the day is running again and the streak goes back.

### S6. Get caught

- When my lead reaches zero, Peck plays, the headline says the goose got me and when, and the
  log buttons are disabled.
- This happens even if I do nothing: the app notices at the time the server said it would.
- The streak resets, and the goose is a gosling again tomorrow.
- A log made earlier that arrives late can still prevent the catch, until the day settles.

### S7. End the day short

- If the day ends with some lead left and a habit unfinished, the headline says how many steps
  I had to spare.
- The streak stays where it was.
- Tomorrow the goose starts that many steps back.
- During the night I can still log for the day that just ended.

### S8. Widgets

- The **Chase widget** shows the scene and the lead, in the small and medium Home Screen sizes
  and on the Lock Screen. It keeps counting down between refreshes.
- The **Feed widget** shows a button for a chunk of each unfinished habit, such as
  "+10 pushups". Tapping one logs it without opening the app, and both widgets redraw.
- On a complete day the widgets say the goose is fed. On a caught day they say it got me.
- Signed out, both widgets ask me to open the app.

### S9. Shortcuts actions

- The Shortcuts app offers three actions: log a habit with an optional amount, undo my last
  log, and get the chase.
- "Log a habit" replies with what was logged and the new lead.
- There are no Siri phrases in v1.

### S10. Log with no signal

- If the server cannot be reached, the log is kept on the phone, and the app, the widget or
  the action says "10 reps logged, they'll count when you're back online".
- The log is sent when the app opens, when the network returns, and from a background task.
- It counts at the time I tapped, not the time it arrived.
- If the day settled before it arrived, the app tells me the log was too late and drops it.
- A log is never counted twice, however many times it is sent.

### S11. Streak and stage

- Today shows my streak and the goose's stage.
- The goose is drawn larger at each stage: gosling, goose, big goose, boss goose, giant goose.
- The stage shows in the app, on the Chase widget and on the public profile.

### S12. Public profile

- In Settings I choose a handle and turn the profile on or off.
- With it on, `/{handle}` shows my display name, the streak and stage, where the goose is
  right now, and the last 35 days as fed, short or caught.
- It never shows habit names, units, amounts, log times or my day's hours.
- With it off, the page is not found.
- A handle someone else has is refused.

### S13. API keys and MCP

- In Settings I can create an API key or an MCP key with a name. The secret is shown once,
  with a copy button, and I can revoke a key.
- For an MCP key the app shows the MCP URL and a `claude mcp add` command to copy.
- With an MCP key, an MCP client can read the chase, list habits, read history, log a habit
  and undo a log. It cannot edit habits, settings or keys.
- A log made through MCP or the API shows in the app the next time it refreshes.
- An MCP key does not work on the REST API, and an API key does not work at `/mcp`.

### S14. Reminders

- In Settings I can turn reminders on, choose the lead below which I am warned, and set quiet
  hours.
- I get at most three a day: when the lead drops below my threshold, an hour before the goose
  would catch me, and an hour before the day ends if I am not done. None arrive in quiet hours.
- A log from any source moves the reminders.
- *The demo shows the server planning and sending these to a fake push service. Delivery to a
  phone waits for an Apple push key.*

### S15. Day and time zone

- In Settings I set when my day starts and ends and my time zone.
- A day shorter than 8 hours or longer than 22 hours is refused.
- A change takes effect tomorrow, and the app shows what is pending.
- When my phone's time zone differs from my setting, the app asks before changing it.

### S16. Delete my account

- In Settings I can delete my account after confirming.
- Every habit, log, day, key and session is removed, and the profile page is not found.

## Not in v1

- An AI assistant inside the app.
- Siri phrases.
- Hosted MCP sign-in (OAuth) for claude.ai and mobile chat apps.
- Pursuers other than the goose.
- Final art. The white goose is a placeholder.
- A Control Center button and an Action button shortcut.
- A Live Activity.
- Working out the chase on the phone when offline.
- Dark mode.
- An iPad layout, an Apple Watch app and localization.
